4.3 For the edited values and the edit log, Customer is the controller of personal data and Publisher is its processor. Publisher processes them only to provide the Offering as Customer configures it, and on Customer's documented instructions. For the service logs and sign-in sessions, Publisher is the controller, as the privacy policy says.

5. Customer responsibilities

Customer is responsible for:

  • a. Database access. Customer's database administrator grants the Offering's application only the rights Customer wants it to have, on the tables Customer chooses. Customer reviews these grants, including column grants. Publisher's setup guidance lists the minimum grants. The service writes only to tables that are enabled for write-back on both sides.
  • b. Backups and recovery. Customer must keep its own backups of every database the Offering writes to, and the means to undo a change. The Offering logs each change but does not back up or restore Customer's data.
  • c. Row-level security in Customer's semantic model, Build permission for Users, and the Power BI tenant settings that the Offering needs. These are set by Customer. The Offering applies the model's own row-level security as the User; it cannot make a wrongly built model safe.
  • d. Content. Customer and its Users are responsible for the accuracy, legality and content of what they enter. Customer must not use free-text columns for special categories of personal data unless it has a legal basis.
  • e. Monitoring of employees. If Customer uses the edit log to monitor workers, Customer is responsible for meeting the law that applies, for example Italian law 300/1970 art. 4.
  • f. Keeping accounts secure: Users' Microsoft accounts, and removing a licence from a person who leaves.
  • g. Checking results. Customer checks results before it relies on them.
